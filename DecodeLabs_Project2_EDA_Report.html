Executive Summary

// Problem → Methodology → Findings → Recommendations

Following the "Executive Summary Architecture" from the training kit, the case is framed before the evidence is presented.

1  Problem Statement

DecodeLabs runs an e-commerce operation across 7 product lines. Leadership needs to know: which products, payment methods, and channels actually drive revenue — and how much of the order pipeline is being lost to cancellations and returns.

2  Methodology

The Project 1 cleaned dataset (1,200 orders, 14 fields, zero missing values, zero duplicates) was profiled with pandas: five-number summaries, skewness, IQR & Z-score outlier screens, and Pearson correlation — then aggregated by product, time, payment method, order status, coupon, and referral source using groupby().

3  Key Findings

  • Order value (TotalPrice) is right-skewed (skew = 0.89) — median (₹823.62) is a truer "typical order" than mean (₹1,053.97).
  • 41.4% of all orders end in Cancelled or Returned status — representing ₹519,673.91 in unrealized/reversed revenue.
  • UnitPrice is the strongest single driver of order value (r = 0.72), ahead of Quantity (r = 0.62).
  • Revenue is well diversified — no single product exceeds 15.5% of total revenue; Chair and Printer are statistically tied for #1.
  • Credit Card orders carry the highest average value (₹1,127.55); Debit Card the lowest (₹1,001.56) — an 11.2% spread.

4  Recommendations

See the full recommendation set at the end of this report, prioritized by expected business impact.

Outlier Detection

// Tools of the Trade — IQR vs. Z-Score

Two independent methods were run against every numeric field to separate noise from signal.

python — IQR method

for col in ['Quantity','UnitPrice','ItemsInCart','TotalPrice']: Q1, Q3 = df[col].quantile([0.25, 0.75]) IQR = Q3 - Q1 lo, hi = Q1 - 1.5*IQR, Q3 + 1.5*IQR outliers = df[(df[col] < lo) | (df[col] > hi)] print(col, len(outliers), f"({len(outliers)/len(df)*100:.2f}%)")

python — Z-score method

z = (df['TotalPrice'] - df['TotalPrice'].mean()) / df['TotalPrice'].std() print("|z| > 3:", (z.abs() > 3).sum())

Diagnosis — The IQR method flags 8 orders (0.67%) as statistically extreme, but the stricter Z-score test (|z|>3) flags zero — meaning these are large but not implausible orders (large-quantity, high-unit-price combinations), not data-entry errors. Per the "Noise vs. Signal" framework: Action = Investigate, not remove. These are candidate VIP/bulk orders worth a closer look, not corrupted rows.

Recommendations

// Actionable Next Steps

Investigate the Cancelled/Returned segment first

It's the single largest revenue leak (41.4% of orders, ₹519,673.91 combined) and skews toward higher-value orders — a root-cause review (payment friction, delivery delays, product mismatch) has the highest expected payoff of any action here.

Report TotalPrice using median, not mean, in stakeholder decks

Given the 0.89 skew, leading with the mean overstates typical order size — pair it with the median for an honest picture, per the "Mean vs. Median" principle.

Build a VIP/bulk-order outreach list from the 8 IQR outliers

Z-score confirms these aren't errors — they're your highest-value legitimate customers, worth a retention or loyalty touchpoint.

Double down on Credit Card and Instagram, but don't abandon the rest

Both lead their categories, but the spread across payment methods (11%) and referral sources (18%) is narrow enough that diversification itself is a strength to protect, not consolidate away from.

Re-run this EDA quarterly as new 2025 data lands

2025 is only a 6-month partial year in this dataset — re-running the same groupby/aggregation code once H2 2025 data arrives will confirm whether the year-over-year revenue decline (₹552K → ₹480K → ₹232K annualized) is a real trend or a seasonality artifact.
